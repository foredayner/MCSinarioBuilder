ARCHITECTURE GUIDE: 흡혈귀곡 - 메이골 (1950s, Gangwon-do mountain village)

PALETTE:
Walls: stripped_spruce_log stacked as log-box walls (귀틀벽), with mud or packed_mud infill and coarse_dirt rubbed into the joints.
Trim and frames: stripped_spruce_log for posts and sills, spruce_planks for door frames, spruce_stairs or spruce_slab for thresholds.
Roofs: spruce_slab and spruce_stairs laid as split-shingle courses (너와), held down by cobblestone, mossy_cobblestone, or andesite stones placed on top.
Foundations: cobblestone or stone_bricks dry-stacked as a low base (돌 기단), with gravel at the footing.
Floors: packed_mud or dirt inside the main room, spruce_planks for the 툇마루 wooden porch, hay_block or brown_carpet for straw mats (멍석).
Windows: white_stained_glass_pane standing in spruce_fence lattice to read as paper panes (창호지), with spruce_trapdoor shutters.
Doors: spruce_door or spruce_trapdoor, with plank-built doors on wooden hinge-style frames.
Chimneys: cobblestone or mud_bricks cone-shaped flues (굴뚝), with a black stone or blackstone cap blackened by soot.
Fences and gates: spruce_fence and spruce_fence_gate woven low as 싸리 울타리, with dead_bush tufts and azalea bits along the base.
Lamps: lantern hung from a spruce_fence post or porch beam; no street lights anywhere in the village.

SHAPES:
Roofs are low, steep-sided gable or hip roofs, pitched around 45 degrees, with a ridge pole of stripped_spruce_log and wide overhangs over the porch.
Storeys: one storey, roughly 4 blocks to the eaves, with the roof taking another 3 to 4 blocks above.
Walls are short and thick; the stone base stands 1 block proud of the ground, floor raised slightly above the dirt yard.
Windows are small and few, about 1x2 with a 2-block spacing, mostly on the front; the back and sides keep closed walls against the cold.
Porches are 툇마루: a narrow spruce_planks platform along the front, 1-2 blocks deep, with a low railing of spruce_fence and a single hanging lantern.
Entrances are low and offset, with a stone step and a door turned away from the prevailing north-west wind.
Decoration is restrained: rice-straw or hay_block bundles stacked under eaves (나뭇단), clay jars (장독대) of terracotta or brown_terracotta beside the back wall, and firewood stacks of stripped_spruce_log.

INTERIORS:
Walls are bare log or mud, lightly whitewashed in places with white_concrete_powder or white_terracotta patches, never painted with modern colours.
Floors are packed earth or stamped mud, covered with straw mats (멍석 or hay_block) and oiled-paper look from brown_carpet.
Furniture is low and handmade: spruce_stairs or spruce_slab for stools, chests of spruce_planks, a low table, a 이불장 cupboard, and sleeping mats along the warm side.
Hearth: a stone 아궁이 built into the kitchen floor, with a cauldron, firewood, and blackened stone walls.
Lighting comes only from oil lamps, 호롱불 in the form of lantern and candle, and the hearth glow; there is no electricity anywhere.

WEAR AND MOOD:
Smoke from the hearths blackens every roof and chimney; use soul_soil dust-darkened blocks, blackstone patches, and cobwebs at eaves.
Moss and lichen spread on north-facing slopes and stones: mossy_cobblestone, moss_carpet, and vines hanging from the roof edge.
Pine needles and resin: spruce_leaves litter the yard, and dark_oak_slab or dark_oak_stairs show sap-stained patches.
Wood greys with age and damp: vary spruce_planks with weathered tones and leave a few planks missing or broken on the porch.
The mood is still and damp, with fog-dim light at dusk, paths of packed dirt and coarse_dirt, and no paved surface anywhere.

BY BUILDING:
House (집): the standard 너와집 described above, with a stone base, a log-box wall, a porch, a hearth, and a yard fenced in 싸리 울타리.
Pyeongsang (평상): an open raised wooden platform of spruce_planks on stone posts beneath a roof of spruce_slab, placed in the open yard as a gathering seat with no walls.
Pine (소나무 당집): a small shrine-shed beneath the great pine at the village centre, built of stripped_spruce_log with a closed roof, a stone altar, and hanging straw ropes (금줄); it is kept dim and sealed with no windows.

AVOID:
Glass windows, real glass panes in any building beyond the shrine's sealed frames.
Clay roof tiles (기와) on common houses, which were for wealthier homes, not 너와 mountain houses.
Brick, concrete, iron, asphalt, or paved stone roads; the village is dirt paths only.
Electric lights, lamp posts, lamps other than oil lanterns and candles, or any modern fixtures.
Western furniture such as beds, chairs with legs, wardrobes with mirrors, or tall tables.
Tall buildings, second storeys with balconies, or any building taller than two storeys.
Japanese-colonial stone offices, Korean hanok courtyard houses with tiled roofs, or red-brick villas.
